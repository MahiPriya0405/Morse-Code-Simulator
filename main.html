What is Morse Code?

Morse code is a communication system that represents letters, numbers and symbols using combinations of dots and dashes. Each character has its own unique pattern, originally designed to be sent as short and long pulses over telegraph lines or radio.

History

Developed in the 1830s-40s by Samuel Morse and Alfred Vail for use on the electrical telegraph, it became the backbone of long-distance communication for over a century.

Uses

Used in maritime distress signals, amateur (ham) radio, aviation navigation beacons, and as an accessible communication method for people with certain disabilities.

Applications

Still taught in military and emergency-response training, and remains a popular hobby among radio operators and puzzle enthusiasts worldwide.
